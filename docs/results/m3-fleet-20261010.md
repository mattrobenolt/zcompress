# M3 fleet run: the flate family (flate, gzip, zlib) against the pinned competitors

Run: `bench-results/20261010T160535Z-m3` (local, gitignored; the run id and
this file are the durable record). Manifest: 7/7 targets, 4 arms, 680 rows
per target, `rounds=5`, `evidence=sufficient` on every row, `errors: {}` on
every target. The two expected tool-scope gaps (libdeflate and zlib-ng carry
no snappy rows) are recorded per target. 36 outlier rounds flagged across
the fleet, reported and never removed.

- Targets: c7i, c8i, c7a, c8a, c7g, c8g, c9g (the seven-model fleet).
- Arms: `zc` (zcompress, HEAD-adjacent worktree), `klauspost/compress`
  v1.18.1-0.20250402062133-8df4d013ff17, `libdeflate` v1.26 (sha-pinned
  tarball), `zlib-ng` 2.3.3 (sha-pinned tarball); the in-binary
  `std.compress.flate` rows (`Container = .gzip/.zlib`, level 1) ride the
  `zc` arm for the std comparison.
- Corpus: `bench/corpus/` — 50 committed files (10 raw shapes: text, random,
  html, rle, mixed × 32K/64K, plus the reference blobs from our encoders),
  deterministic, hashed per round (`jsonl.py` verifies the hash on ingest).
- Protocol: seeded balanced Latin-square rounds under last-core CPU
  isolation; exact Mann-Whitney/Wilcoxon intervals (fastmem's estimator
  core); A/A noise floors per target.
- Method note: the container-vs-core deltas compare the in-binary paired
  rows (the code-layout-interference finding — cross-binary rows are not
  comparable on latency-bound shapes).

Raw flate compression is 2-3x klauspost's and 3-4x the C libraries'; zlib
compression (the vector Adler-32) beats every pinned competitor on every
target. gzip compression is ahead of klauspost on the Intel targets and
behind on ARM (their hardware CRC32 writes the trailer nearly free).

The raw flate decode core is 3-4x klauspost's and std's, mid-pack against
libdeflate and zlib-ng — a first-pass decoder holding its own against the
tuned C set. The containers pay for what they verify: every gzip-decode
competitor on these targets checksums with hardware CRC32 (`crc32x` /
PCLMULQDQ), and zc's slice-by-8 software kernel plus the streaming-reader
ride turns a 0.3x core into a 2-5.5x container. zlib decode is the split
case: 2-3x ahead of klauspost's scalar Adler everywhere, ahead of zlib-ng
on the Graviton targets (the vector kernel maps to their NEON), behind on
the A/I targets.

### The container overhead, isolated (in-binary paired rows)

gzip: 1.78x the paired flate core on compress, 3.14x on decompress.
zlib: 1.28x on compress, 2.31x on decompress. The compress side is the
checksum pass alone; the decompress side adds the streaming-reader ride
over the one-shot (~3 µs per 32K, the perf wave's measurement).

### A/A noise floors

1.0000-1.0004 across all targets and codecs — the estimator's noise floor
at n=5 with the Latin-square protocol.

## What this run decides

- The deferred comptime per-model CRC selection (docs/zcompress-plan.md,
  "Milestones"/"Decisions deferred") now has its fleet data: the gzip
  decode gap against every hardware-CRC competitor (2-7.5x) is the CRC
  kernel plus the streaming ride. The route is ARM `crc32x` / x86
  PCLMULQDQ via comptime selection — the plan's `ghash_polyval` study
  first, no inline asm before a vector route is proven slower.
- The streaming-reader overhead (~3 µs per 32K) is the second fleet-wave
  target (a same-shape decode-side ride without the container framing).
- No regression action for compression: zc leads the pinned set on flate
  and zlib and is within noise-to-ahead of klauspost on gzip (Intel ahead,
  ARM behind on their hardware CRC).
